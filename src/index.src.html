    Philosophy

    Think first. Then look.

    ExamiQ is built on a few simple commitments about how understanding forms, and every lesson is designed around them.

    01

    Commit to a prediction.

    Every lesson starts by asking what you think will happen, before anything is shown. A guess gives the result something to confirm or overturn.

    02

    Mistakes are data.

    A wrong prediction is not a fail state. You still launch, still watch, and the gap between what you expected and what happened is where the lesson is.

    03

    Make the mechanism visible.

    Trajectories, vectors and live readouts show why a result happens, not just the answer, and you can change the conditions yourself.

    04

    Prove it to yourself.

    Recall the principle without the simulation in front of you, then apply it to a situation you have not seen before.

    The learning workflow

    Five stages, one arc.

    This is the loop the Projectile Motion lesson runs today, and the template for every lesson that follows.

    01 / PREDICT

    Commit.

    Choose the launch angle you think gives the longest range, before seeing anything.

    02 / OBSERVE

    Test it.

    Launch your angle and at least one other, and compare where each one lands.

    03 / SPATIAL LAB

    Explore.

    Change speed, angle, aim and air resistance in a 3-D lab and watch the path respond.

    04 / RECALL

    Remember.

    Answer a question on the key principle from memory, with feedback on your choice.

    05 / TRANSFER

    Apply.

    A bonus challenge puts the same idea into a new situation.
